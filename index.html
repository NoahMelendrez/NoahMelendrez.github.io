A Green New Deal for
Houston

↓

Justice must be restorative, not punitive. We must end for-profit prisons and all penal labor. We should demilitarize the police, reorienting towards public service with officers recruited from the neighbors they work in. Houston should create a Holistic Assistance Response Team (HART) similar to Harris County and funded with the police budget like in Austin. The city should recommit to the Vision Zero program to eliminate traffic deaths, which disproportionately affect people of color. Houston and Harris county should invest more in libraries, staying open in the evenings and Sundays. Alternatives to animal kill shelters should be prioritized.

We call for the abolishment of ICE and the removal of the border wall. Houston should end all collaboration between its police department and ICE, including refusing to enter into a 287(g) agreement. Additionally, the city should create an Immigrant Legal Services Fund similar to Harris County’s, which should be expanded. All Houstonians should be made aware of their rights, both online and through community teach-ins. All city and county departments should accept library cards as a valid form of ID. Houston and Harris County should cancel all ALPR mass surveillance contracts and remove all existing cameras.

Education, from Pre-K to college, should be universally available to all, as well as child care. All education jobs should be required to be union jobs, and teachers should have the right to collectively bargain. Private & charter schools should be made illegal. A National Program for Accelerated Reading should be created to combat falling literacy rates. The HISD takeover must be reversed. Superintendents should be democratically elected by their community, not appointed by the state. Special education programs should be expanded.

The U.S. should cease all offensive operations, including blockades, embargoes, and sanctions. All offensive and defensive weapons sales to Israel should cease, in particular those shipped from the Port of Houston.

Everyone is entitled to a job, every job should pay a living wage, and workers deserve a say in how their workplace is run. City contractors should be required to pay a $23/hr minimum wage, and to offer worker’s compensation, healthcare and childcare benefits for all city-contracted workers and their dependents. Contractors that have engaged in wage theft or OSHA violations should be blacklisted from any future contract offers. Houston City College should create a workforce union job pipeline program. Municipal city internet should be provided to reflect the necessity of internet access in the 21st century. Businesses should be required to offer paid family leave. Public companies with over one thousand employees must have 30% board representation composed of members voted by the workers to represent their interests.

Everyone has a right to a home. Displaced tenants should be provided relocation assistance. Eviction should not be allowed without representation. Notice of tenants rights in the spoken language of the tenant should be required with eviction notices. Junk fees, including pet rent, should be made illegal. Landlords should be required to register with the city. There should be a fine for deposit theft, as well as for positive black mold tests. Houston should pass an empty homes tax, as well as raise taxes on out of state property owners. Houston should pass a Fair Housing Ordinance to prevent discrimination against any group of people, and repeal the Food Sharing Ordinance. Instead of buyouts, Houston should provide elevation assistance for at-risk homes. The Houston Housing Authority should be expanded.

Everyone has a right to healthcare, including mental, dental, and vision, as well as family planning. We should invest more in medical research and disease prevention.

The Strong Mayor system must be replaced with a more powerful city council. Community members, workers, and union members should be appointed to their respective board chairs. End tax abatements for corporations. A database of city-owned parcels should be developed, and flood prone parcels should be held in a land bank as a publicly controlled asset, and the decision on how to develop these lands should be voted on by the community.

Our economy must divest from fossil fuels, while creating good union jobs in the process. In the meantime, workers in the fossil fuel industry should have greater protections, especially in the Permian Basin. FEMA must be fully funded, and cuts to the CDC must be undone to bring back safer food regulations. The government must subsidize community investment in solar energy and other renewable sources.There should be an indefinite moratorium on data center construction until a system can be created wherein residents participate in the decision to construct any new centers, as well as sharing in profits.

Funding for Resilience Hubs should be subsidized by CenterPoint Energy in exchange for continuing their city contract. Harris and the surrounding counties must install and maintain real-time air quality control sensors with an alert system. Any industrial facility seeking a city contract or infrastructure permit must disclose their emissions data and keep to standards outlined in city ordinances or contracts. Eminent domain should be utilized against repeat offenders. Recycling should be city-run, and a composting program should be created. Trees for Houston should also be city-funded.

Sidewalks must be formally made the responsibility of the city, with a property owner fee for their maintenance. The city should fund a bicycle rideshare program and invest in bike infrastructure. Houston should minimum parking requirements for new construction, and expand parking benefit districts to increase investment in local infrastructure. METRO should pilot a fare free program for one month. MetroNEXT should be implemented as originally voted on, including the voter approved Gulfton Bus Rapid Transit. The Green Line should be expanded to connect to Hobby Airport. Houston should commit to road diets to reduce pollution and allow for more bike and pedestrian infrastructure.
