A Day Without
Capitalism

Think about your average day. You wake up early, too early. You get in your car to start your lengthy commute, you’re almost out of gas. Groan. It’s super expensive. The war abroad has ballooned the gas prices. It’s a 45-minute commute in bumper to bumper traffic on 610. They recently added another lane and it has only made the situation worse.

At work, your boss yells at you for missing a deadline that wasn’t communicated. You want to say something, but you need to keep this job. Your partner got sick from living next to a refinery and depends on your health insurance to get the care they need to survive. Your boss is still yelling at you. You grin and bear it. You count down the minutes until your shift ends.

At last you clock out. It’s been a long day. On your way home you hit a pothole and pop a tire. Your finances for the month are ruined.

At home you start making dinner. You throw out the spinach in your freezer, you heard on the radio that all frozen vegetables have been recalled. You look at your guitar, it’s dusty. You haven’t picked it up in ages. You look at your partner, it feels like it’s been forever since you’ve had a date night. You close your eyes and start to dream.

You wake up to the sounds of birds chirping, the air smells fresh and not like a tire fire. You get ready for work and take the train. It's a short ride through a sea of greenery. You manage to read a few pages of your book. You get to work, you have a 32-hour work week, and you got a raise! Your union got it through negotiations last month. It’s a good job, but if you ever need a change of pace or want to try something else, you have the freedom to do that. You and your partner are covered under Medicare for All. It’s time to go home. You have dinner, it’s full of fresh fruits and vegetables, and they’re safe to eat! There hasn’t been a recall in years. You and your partner spend the afternoon at the Bayou. You have a picnic, pick up your guitar, and play your partner a song you wrote. The sun is setting, but no worries, you have a 3-day weekend.

There’s no ICE, no fear of police, just the freedom to pursue your hobbies, enjoy time with your friends and family.

A better world is possible.
A better Houston is possible.
We just have to fight for it.
For all of us.

Houston is a city of immigrants. More than a fourth of Houstonians in the Greater Houston Area are immigrants. Immigrants have built our city’s streets and homes, care for our children and are an integral part of what gives Houston its multicultural character, diversity, and incredible food scene. ICE and the Trump administration’s mass deportation agenda has created a climate of terror and fear that is felt in homes, schools, and hospitals. A climate that makes workers fear for their lives on their way to work, that makes children fear they will be separated from their families. This is a national emergency and we cannot allow one more murder at the hands of ICE. We will abolish and prosecute ICE.

  • Abolish and prosecute ICE
  • Close all ICE Detention Internment Camps
  • End family detention
  • No more Border Wall
  • Fund Immigrant Legal Defense
  • End HPD Collaboration with ICE
  • Refuse to enter in 287(g) agreement
  • Expanded Library Card accepted at all city departments as a valid form of ID
  • Regular Know Your Rights Community Teach-Ins
  • No More Mass Surveillance
    • Cancel all mass surveillance contracts (Flock, vigilant solutions, etc.)
    • Take down the cameras!

  • Free college

  • Universal Pre-K
